05 · Responsive

Built for the fight.

The time from your click to the shot on screen, measured in the game. The view turns the frame you move the mouse, your gun is drawn from the newest moment, and on OpenH1's own servers your input lands on a step up to 120 times a second instead of 30. Here is one click, slowed down.

8× more responsive than the original.

OpenH1 fires 6 ms after you click. The original took 48 ms. In a close fight, that's who shoots first. And on OpenH1's own servers, your input reaches everyone 120 times a second.

Click, tap or press Space to fireTimed from our measurements

Where the time goes

Pressing fire, stage by stage. The original drew one frame a tick and queued it behind the display; OpenH1 acts on your press at the next step and shows it in the next frame.

  • Original timingone frame a tick, recreated47.7 ms
  • Legacy 30plays with OpenCE19.7 ms
  • OpenH1 60 TickOpenH1's own servers11.1 ms
  • OpenH1 120 TickOpenH1's own servers5.9 ms
  • Reading the press
  • Waiting for the tick
  • Tick to its frame
  • Drawing
  • Handing the frame on (the original's queue)

Measured with the game's own log (debug.latency_log): pressing fire to the frame handed to the screen, median of 21 presses (50 for the original), on a Mac, in a local game with bots. Your display adds its own few milliseconds to both. The original's timing is recreated in OpenH1 with its improvements switched off: 30 ticks, one frame a tick, no frames between ticks, the view drawn from the last tick. Each stage is its own median. This is a local game: on the Xbox's system link, which ran in lockstep, your own moves also waited a round trip to the other machines.

09 · Under the hood

For the purists.

What makes it the original, and what it adds. Every line is in the project's documentation.

Code
The Xbox game's decompiled C, compiled natively, with few and marked changesNot emulation. Xbox build 2342.
Simulation
30 ticks a second, as shippedThe same calculations on every machine. The scorecard holds every change bit-identical at 30 Hz, tick by tick.
60 / 120 Hz
Sub-steps for what you feel in your hands, offline and on OpenH1 60 and 120 Tick serversEvery rate plays exactly as 30 does
Frames
One per refresh of your displayEach frame drawn between the last two ticks; nothing the game calculates changes
Architecture
A 64-bit program, with the game compiled as a 32-bit-pointer guest inside itILP32: arm64_32 on ARM, x32 on x86-64
Renderers
Vulkan 1.3 on Windows, Linux, Android and in VR; Metal on the MacOne command stream into both
Picture
SMAA or FXAA, anisotropic filtering, sharper shadows, per-pixel lightingOne switch: Video Setup › Graphics › Preset, Classic or Enhanced
Lights
Per-pixel, inverse-square, in linear light, with a glow on every boltPlaced where their objects are drawn, between ticks
Display
Any resolution from 640 × 480, a field of view up to 150°, a HUD redrawn at 8×
Network
Legacy games play online with OpenCE players; OpenH1 60 and 120 Tick games send every sub-step of your inputInternet play needs no servers to set up
Mods
Texture, sound, text and model packs from the game's Mods menu; Custom Edition maps that share themselves

10 · Your copy

Bring your own game.

OpenH1 contains none of the game's data. You need your own Xbox disc image of the game. Any version works.

  1. Download the game for your machine, below.
  2. Start the game and choose your disc image (.iso or .xiso), or its maps folder, when it asks. How to get your maps.
  3. It copies out its maps once. Then you're in.

Steam Deck

And any 64-bit Linux PC with Vulkan.

Download for Steam Deck
  1. In Desktop Mode, download it and unzip it.
  2. Open install.sh in that folder, and choose your game's disc image or maps folder when it asks. Or skip that: put the disc image in Downloads or on your SD card.
  3. Play OpenH1 from Game Mode. It finds the image.
